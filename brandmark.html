Brand Mark Guidelines
The Mark

One river, one goal.

The SoloTelos mark fuses the letters S and T into a single image of the investor's journey: a silver river (S) rising and threading the stem of a slate-blue T — the goal, the telos. Where the current crosses the stem twice, it quietly reads as a dollar sign.

The River — S

The self-directed path: winding, tapering from a thin source to a wide mouth, tilted 10° to give it forward motion. Rendered in silver as a flowing ribbon, never a rigid letter.

The Goal — T

Τέλος begins with tau. The upright slate stem is the destination the river climbs toward — fixed, steady, the aim every feature serves.

The Thread — $

The river crosses the stem twice, forming a subtle dollar sign — the financial purpose — and binding S and T into one solo mark.

The Tilt — 10°

The river leans right; the T stays plumb. Motion against stability. The tilt is fixed — the mark is never drawn upright or at another angle.

Primary Lockup

Mark + wordmark.

The horizontal lockup is the default signature. "Solo" in Montserrat Light, "Telos" in Philosopher Bold, tinted the slate brand color. Keep the mark's height equal to the cap height of the wordmark.

Usage

Do & don't.

Do

  • Keep the T upright and the river tilted 10°.
  • Use slate + silver on dark; graphite for the river on light.
  • Add a hairline slate border when placing on busy or mid-tone backgrounds.
  • Preserve clear space equal to the T crossbar height.
  • Use slate for every interactive accent — buttons, links, CTAs.

Don't

  • Don't recolor the mark or apply gradients.
  • Don't straighten, over-tilt, or rotate the whole mark.
  • Don't place the slate T on pure black without a border at small sizes.
  • Don't stretch, condense, or reproportion the letters.
  • Don't separate the river from the stem — they read as one.
Typography

Montserrat & Philosopher.

Montserrat carries UI and body; Philosopher — a Greek-derived serif — carries "Telos" and display headings, echoing the classical root of the name.
